## 5. Pay frequency, deduction and interest constraints

W1 art.116 originally distinguishes labourers (twice monthly, ≤16-day intervals)
from employees (at least monthly). W2 art.1 subsequently requires covered
workers/employees twice monthly: second week50% net monthly wage; fourth week
remaining wages/benefits. This is the precise source conflict with an argument
for monthly payment based only on1997 wording. No monthly10th exception or “few
days late” legal grace was verified. Preserve the owner's monthly comparison
policy and both twice-monthly modes, with the conflict visible; two independently
calculated periods do not automatically meet the50% net requirement.

W1 arts.126–129 forbid recruitment deductions and restrict employer set-off;
cash advances use gradual recovery within attachable/transferable wages. Art.128
distinguishes already-earned wage instalments, recoverable from the next payment,
from lending. Art.130 uses monthly marginal limits above guaranteed minimum wage:
20% between1× and3× minimum,30% between3× and10×,50% above10×; the minimum is
protected. **The applicable minimum, wage base and interaction with other
deductions are unresolved for this business.** Do not substitute a universal
20/30/50%-of-net cap, use a sector minimum without applicability, or treat$100 as
a legal limit. Manager consent does not resolve these constraints.

C1 art.583 requires an interest agreement; art.585 limits agreed interest,
counts certain fees as interest, and treats excess paid interest as principal
repayment. Art.586(2) provides statutory capitalization after **more than one
year's interest in arrears and notice followed by nonpayment**. This does not
verify selectable automatic monthly compounding. A current Cambodian maximum
rate and validity of contractual monthly capitalization/rate changes have **not**
been established. Leave numeric legal ceilings unset; obtain Cambodian legal
review of lending terms separately from accountant tax confirmation.

Below-market lending: T1 art.15(2), item6, treats the shortfall against the MEF
market rate as a fringe benefit. L1 permits the employer's qualifying third-party
minimum or GDT **N−1** benchmark. L2 supplies2025 annual9.36%KHR/8.45%USD; using
these as the2026 N−1 benchmark is a supported reading of L1, requiring documented
company-method confirmation. These are **tax valuation benchmarks, not lending
ceilings or mandatory employee interest**. FBT=`20% × assessed taxable benefit`;
valuation balance/day basis and tax-inclusive gross-up need confirmation. Do not
invent `annualRate/12 × openingPrincipal` as a legal default. The owner's first
three zero-interest months may still create FBT; zero contractual interest does
not imply zero employer tax.
